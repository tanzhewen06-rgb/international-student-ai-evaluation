**On the "quiet one is my place" thought:** I'd gently push back. You're in your first few weeks of a first semester in a second language — being slower to speak *right now* is what early language immersion looks like, not a fixed identity. The fact that you understand the lab steps when you read them alone is important evidence: your comprehension is fine; it's the speed of spoken production under pressure that's still developing. That develops with practice and time, not by accepting a permanent role.

Two things worth noticing about your current practice:
- Practicing the *same three sentences* and still getting stuck suggests the sentences themselves may be too long or too complicated. Try shorter chunks, said aloud, out loud, slowly.
- Practicing alone doesn't rehearse the hard part — speaking *to* people. Even one low-stakes run-through with a friend, or recording yourself and listening back, trains a different skill than silent repetition.

**On the group:** you can ask for a speaking part *without* making it a confrontation. Something like: "I'd like to take a speaking part in the presentation — could I have [specific slide or section]? I'm happy to keep doing the slides too." That's a reasonable request, and if the rubric shows speaking is individually marked, it's also just protecting your own grade.
